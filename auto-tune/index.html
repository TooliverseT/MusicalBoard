Free online auto-tune — vocal pitch correction in your browser, no upload. Correct a dry vocal to any key and scale with formant-preserving pitch correction. Record or upload in the header Controller, press Apply, compare on the pitch roadmap, then export with Download Auto-Tune (MP3, WAV, OGG, or M4A), Save to take into slot A–D, or from the Controller download menu.

Your voice never leaves your device

Everything runs locally using WebAssembly. The audio stays in your browser tab and is never sent to a server. A slow connection does not add queue time. Longer takes take longer to analyze, and the progress bar follows that work.

Hear what your take sounds like in tune — without a DAW

This won't replace Auto-Tune Pro or Melodyne on a complex master. It uses TD-PSOLA so your vocal color stays closer to your voice while pitch moves toward the scale. Use it to hear a phrase corrected to a key, spot flat notes, or try Gentle and Natural presets when you want a light pass — not a robotic snap.

Play the original and the corrected version back to back

After Apply, use Load Original and Load Auto-Tuned to switch versions in the header player at the same timestamp. The correction stays on this page until you press Save to take, choose a slot, and name it. The pitch roadmap shows the original trace in gray and the corrected trace in pink. Click the chart to move the playhead.

Download the corrected file without leaving the browser

After Apply, use Download Auto-Tune beside Re-apply: pick Format (MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A), then download. Filenames use the take’s recording time, e.g. autotune_2025-10-03_14-30-00.mp3. Or press Save to take, pick slot A–D, and export that slot from the Controller download menu — same local encode, no upload.

How to use it

Record a take using the Controller strip in the header, or upload an existing audio file. Once a recording is loaded, the controls below become active. Set your key and scale — or pick a preset — then press Apply Auto-Tune. The corrected file is generated in your browser. Nothing is uploaded. A dry vocal is the right input; a full mix gets pitch-shifted as a whole.

Runs locally — your audio never leaves your browser

  • Audio is processed entirely in the browser tab. Nothing is sent to a server.
  • Nothing is uploaded, so connection speed does not add waiting. A long take still needs time to analyze.
  • The trade-off: browser-based pitch correction can't match dedicated studio plugins on complex vocal runs or rapid syllables. For practice, demos, and intonation checks, it's more than enough.
  • Your Key, Scale, and slider settings are saved in your browser's local storage and restored next time you visit.

What each control actually does to your voice

  • Key — Tell the tool what key the song is in. If you're singing a C major melody, pick C. Getting this wrong means your notes will be pulled toward the wrong pitches — you'll hear it immediately. If you're not sure of the key, set Scale to Chromatic: it snaps each note to the nearest semitone without forcing it into a specific key.
  • Scale — Defines which notes are allowed after correction. Major is the bright, "happy" sound most Western pop uses. Minor (natural or harmonic) is darker and more dramatic. Pentatonic is the five-note scale behind most rock, R&B, and soul leads — it's hard to go wrong with it. Blues adds the blue note for that characteristic bent feeling. Chromatic doesn't restrict anything, just moves each pitch to the closest half step.
  • Strength (0–100%) — How far the center of each note moves toward the scale. At 100% the center sits on the target note. Vibrato and scoops are not removed here; Retune Speed does that. Around 60–75% the center moves most of the way. Below 40% it barely moves. The first visit starts at 75%. A saved setting in this browser is kept.
  • Retune Speed (0–100%) — How fast that center arrives, and how much vibrato stays. 0% snaps at once and flattens vibrato — the robotic sound. 100% eases in and keeps vibrato and short ornaments. The slider is labeled Snap on the left and Vibrato on the right. 50–70% is the natural range.
  • Tolerance (0–100¢) — A dead zone around each target note. Notes that land within this distance (in cents — hundredths of a semitone) are left completely alone. Only pitches further away get corrected. This is useful for blues and jazz where slight pitch bends are intentional: raise Tolerance to 30–50¢ and those deliberate inflections won't get snapped flat.

Before you hit Apply

  • You need an active take from the header Controller — record, upload, or select slot A–D in Takes and backing. Apply stays grayed out until that take is loaded and playback is paused.
  • If you don't know the key, Chromatic is a safe default. It corrects pitch without forcing a specific tonal center.
  • Presets — Gentle, Natural, Moderate (Default), Tight, and Hard set Strength, Retune Speed, and Tolerance together. Gentle is a light pass: Strength 30%, Retune Speed 95%, Tolerance 20¢, so only a little of the drift moves and vibrato stays. Moderate (Default) is 75% / 55% / 25¢, the same start a first visit uses. Tight is 95% / 25% / 10¢: notes land on pitch for a polished pop sound, without the stepped robotic effect of Hard. Sliders still override a preset. If this browser already saved settings, those values load instead.
  • A good starting point when nothing is saved yet: Strength 75%, Retune Speed 55%, Tolerance 25%.

After processing

  • Intonation readout — average distance to the nearest scale tone, measured on the original and again on the corrected audio.
  • Pitch roadmap — gray is the original pitch, pink is the pitch measured on the corrected file. Click the chart to move the playhead. Gaps are unvoiced sections (consonants, breath).
  • Load Original / Load Auto-Tuned — pushes either version into the Controller player so you can compare them at the same timestamp.
  • Re-apply Auto-Tune — change Key, Scale, sliders, or preset and run again. Always starts from the original audio; corrections do not stack.
  • Download Auto-Tune — appears in the action row after Apply: Download Auto-Tune button, then Format (MP3 → WAV → OGG → M4A). Saves the corrected file locally; filename uses the take recording timestamp (autotune_YYYY-MM-DD_HH-MM-SS.ext).
  • Save to take — after Apply, stores the corrected audio in a slot A–D with a name you choose. It does not replace the dry recording in another slot unless you overwrite that slot on purpose.
  • Controller export — after Save to take, open Download in the header, pick that slot, and choose MP3, WAV, OGG, or M4A (WebM is for mic captures, not re-encoded corrections).

Who this is actually for

  • Singers who want to hear a practice take corrected and see which notes keep landing flat or sharp.
  • Songwriters who record rough demos and want a quick pitch cleanup before sharing.
  • Anyone curious what their voice sounds like with pitch correction, without buying a plugin.
  • Voice teachers who want to demonstrate the before/after of intonation work in a lesson.
